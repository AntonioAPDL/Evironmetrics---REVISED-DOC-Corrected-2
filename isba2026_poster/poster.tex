\SectionHead{Question}{Fast correction before synthesis}

\begin{KeyQuestion}
{\SmallBodyFont\bfseries\color{TitleSlate}
Can historical product archives learn source discrepancies, propagate those
corrections into new quantile-level ensemble forecasts, and synthesize
calibrated posterior predictions fast enough for real-time product updates?\par}
\end{KeyQuestion}

\vspace{0.20cm}
{\SmallBodyFont
Case study: daily San Lorenzo River flow at the USGS Big Trees gauge on the
\(\log(1+x)\) scale. Before each origin, USGS flow and retrospective
GloFAS/NWS products learn dynamic source discrepancies relative to the gauge.
After the origin, issued ensembles and exogenous covariates carry those
corrections into source-adjusted quantile forecasts and one posterior predictive
flow distribution.\par}

\SectionHead{Model}{Main Workflow}
\PosterModelSchematic
\FigureCaption[Source-aware exDQLM workflow.]{For each target quantile \(p_0\),
pre-origin USGS observations and retrospective product histories identify a
latent river-flow quantile and source-specific correction states. After the
cutoff, issued ensemble forecasts and staged exogenous covariates propagate
corrected forecast-window quantiles. The seven fitted quantile lanes are then
synthesized into one posterior predictive distribution and scored only with
held-out USGS observations.}

\vspace{0.34cm}
\begin{SoftNote}
  {\SmallBodyFont\bfseries\color{TitleSlate}extended Dynamic Quantile Linear Model\par}
  \vspace{0.08cm}
  {\CaptionFont\color{MutedSlate}
  For each target quantile \(p_0\), let \(T\) denote the forecast
  origin/cutoff. The extended Dynamic Quantile Linear Model (exDQLM) links observed flow,
  retrospective product histories, and issued forecast members through one
  latent river-flow quantile. Product-family discrepancies learn source
  corrections before the origin; the same source channels, with forecast-window
  covariates \(\mathbf{x}_{T+k}\), then define corrected predictive quantiles
  after the origin.\par}
  \vspace{-0.20cm}
  \noindent\resizebox{0.995\linewidth}{!}{%
  \begin{minipage}{1.13\linewidth}
    {\ModelEqFont\color{PosterInk}
    \setlength{\jot}{0.045cm}
    \begin{alignat*}{3}
    \text{Observed measurements:}\quad&
    y_t^o
    &\sim{}& \operatorname{exAL}_{p_0}\!\left(\mathbf{F}_t' \boldsymbol{\theta}_t + \zeta_t, \sigma^o, \gamma^o\right),
    &\qquad& 1 \le t \le T,
    \\
    \text{Retrospective products:}\quad&
    z_t^j
    &\sim{}& \operatorname{exAL}_{p_0}\!\left(\mathbf{F}_t'(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t + \boldsymbol{\delta}_t^j) + \zeta_t, \sigma^j, \gamma^j\right),
    &\qquad& j \in \mathcal{J},\ 1 \le t \le T,
    \\
    \text{Forecast products:}\quad&
    y_T^{j,i}(k)
    &\sim{}& \operatorname{exAL}_{p_0}\!\left(\mathbf{F}_{T+k}'(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{T+k} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{T+k}^j) + \zeta_{T+k}, \sigma^j, \gamma^j\right),
    &\qquad& j \in \mathcal{J},\ i \in \mathcal{I}_j,\ k \in \mathcal{K}_j,
    \\
    \text{Trend and seasonal states:}\quad&
    \boldsymbol{\theta}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\theta}_{t-1}
    &\sim{}& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{G}_t \boldsymbol{\theta}_{t-1}, \mathbf{W}_t^\theta\right),
    &\qquad& 1 \le t \le T+K_{\max},
    \\
    \text{Source correction states:}\quad&
    \boldsymbol{\delta}_t^j \mid \boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}^j
    &\sim{}& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{G}_t \boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}^j, \mathbf{W}_t^{\delta^j}\right),
    &\qquad& j \in \mathcal{J},\ 1 \le t \le T+K_{\max},
    \\
    \text{Exogenous adjustment:}\quad&
    \zeta_t \mid \zeta_{t-1}, \boldsymbol{\psi}_{t-1}
    &\sim{}& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\lambda \zeta_{t-1} + \mathbf{x}_t'\boldsymbol{\psi}_{t-1}, w_t^\zeta\right),
    &\qquad& 1 \le t \le T+K_{\max},
    \\
    \text{Covariate-adjustment states:}\quad&
    \boldsymbol{\psi}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\psi}_{t-1}
    &\sim{}& \mathcal{N}\!\left(\boldsymbol{\psi}_{t-1}, \mathbf{W}_t^\psi\right),
    &\qquad& 1 \le t \le T+K_{\max}.
    \end{alignat*}
    }
  \end{minipage}%
  }
  \vspace{-0.18cm}
  {\FootFont\color{MutedSlate}
  \(\operatorname{exAL}_{p_0}\) is the extended asymmetric Laplace working
  likelihood for target quantile \(p_0\); \(\sigma\) and \(\gamma\) are
  source-specific scale and skewness terms. \(T\) is the forecast origin, \(j\)
  a product family, \(i\) an ensemble member, \(k\) a lead, and \(K_{\max}\) the
  longest horizon. The shared quantile state is \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_t\);
  source discrepancies/corrections are \(\boldsymbol{\delta}_t^j\); and
  \((\zeta_t,\boldsymbol{\psi}_t)\) is the exogenous-adjustment block driven by
  covariates \(\mathbf{x}_t\) such as precipitation, soil moisture, and the GDPC
  climate-index summary. Component discounting controls the evolution
  variances. The DQLM comparison in the Results panel is the conjugate
  AL-likelihood baseline; the selected exDQLM uses exAL and updates the
  non-conjugate \(\sigma,\gamma\) terms with VB-LD.\par}
\end{SoftNote}

\SectionHead{Takeaways}{Main takeaways}
\begin{KeyQuestion}

  \begin{itemize}
    \setlength{\itemsep}{0.16cm}
    \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
    \item Historical archives learn source-specific corrections before new
    forecast products are blended.
    \item The selected exDQLM extends the conjugate DQLM with exAL scale and
    skewness flexibility.
    \item Seven corrected quantile lanes synthesize one posterior predictive
    distribution.

    origins; DQLM leads at the final origin.
  \end{itemize}\par}
